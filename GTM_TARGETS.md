# Target list: first client

Working GTM doc. The goal is **one reference customer**, not revenue. Ranked by
who can say yes without a meeting, which is not the same as who has the biggest
gap.

## The two columns that decide order

**Hook** comes from our own analysis of their catalog. Every one is checkable by
that brand in their own admin in under a minute, which is the entire point: it
proves work was done before the email was sent.

**Who decides** is the constraint nobody optimises for. A brand where one person
owns the site can say yes to a free pilot in a reply. A brand with a Director of
Ecommerce behind three layers cannot, no matter how good the hook.

> **Everything in the "who decides" column is unverified.** It is my impression
> of these companies and it may be stale or wrong. Verify each before sending
> anything. Do not treat any of it as fact, and do not let me generate contact
> details: fabricated names and emails are worse than no list.

## Getting from 14 to 40

Fourteen is not a pipeline. Three ways to expand, cheapest first:

1. **Vastraa's own affiliate catalog.** Whatever brands the app already indexes
   are brands we can analyse the same way, and there is a real relationship or
   at least a real reason to be in the inbox. **This is the best source and it
   costs nothing.**
2. **Adjacent DTC apparel on Shopify.** Same shape as the 14 above. The analysis
   runs unchanged on any catalog we can legitimately obtain.
3. **Referrals from anyone who replies**, including the ones who say no. A brand
   that declines will often name a peer who has the problem worse.

## The email

One brand per email, no template feel. Four sentences:

1. Who you are, in your own right: you run a styling app that indexes their catalog.
2. The hook, as a fact about them, not a claim about you.
3. What it means for a shopper, in one line.
4. The ask: 200 products, one week, free, no strings.

Do not attach a report. Do not explain the pipeline. Do not mention accuracy,
vision models, or verifiers. The email exists to get one reply.

## What counts as a result

- **A reply, of any kind** — the hook is either interesting or it isn't
- **A file** — someone sends products; the terms problem disappears the moment they do
- **A no with a reason** — worth more than silence, and often names the real buyer

Send to **all of tier 1 and 2** at once. Nine emails is still thin for reading a
signal, but it is enough to tell whether the hook lands, and the reply text will
teach more than the reply rate.

## What not to do next

Not more measurement. Not a landing page. Not pricing. Not a demo. The reason
there is no customer is that nobody has been asked.
